# Paleo SMB of the Northern Hemisphere ice sheets: literature (LGM, deglaciation, MIS3)

A review made for the chion-BESSI SMB tuning on imposed PaleoMIST geometry with the PaleoEmulator climate
(2026-11-12). "Read" means seen in the abstract, full text or tables; "inferred" means a conversion or
interpretation (1 mm SLE ≈ 362 Gt). Not read in full: Ullman et al. 2015 and Niu et al. 2024 (paywalled), Gandy et
al. 2023 (blocked).

## 1. SMB on imposed geometries through the deglaciation

**Kapsch et al. 2021** (TC 15, 1131, doi:10.5194/tc-15-1131-2021; read) — the only fully transient, imposed-geometry
energy-balance SMB of all NH ice sheets found.
- MPI-ESM1.2-CR (ECHAM6.3 T31, MPIOM 3°), 26–0 ka, prescribed GLAC-1D ice and meltwater (ICE-6G in an appendix).
- Offline 3-D energy and mass balance: 24 elevation classes (0–8000 m) with height corrections of T, humidity,
  precipitation, LW and pressure; 5-layer snow with percolation, refreezing and runoff; albedo with ageing, snow depth,
  density and a cloud term (≤ 0.1); tuned to MAR/RACMO over Greenland 1980–2010.
- Greenland: SMB ≈ 380 Gt/yr at the LGM (21–19 ka), melt ≈ 0; accumulation ~420 → ~670 Gt/yr by 15 ka; minimum
  216 Gt/yr at 8.7 ka; highest ELA 1556 m at 9.3 ka.
- Other ice sheets: ELA only (rising continuously from 21 ka), no integrated SMB. GLAC-1D and ICE-6G "qualitatively
  similar". Balance of the imposed geometry not assessed.
- Stated limits: melt overestimated on western margins by the downscaling; resolution-dependent biases; no height
  feedback.
- Follow-ups: Kapsch et al. 2022 (GRL, doi:10.1029/2021GL096767; ocean response set by the reconstruction and the
  meltwater method); Mikolajewicz et al. 2025 (CP 21, 719, doi:10.5194/cp-21-719-2025; interactive mPISM + VILMA, same
  EBM with hourly forcing): NH ice volume at ~25 ka below ICE-6G/GLAC-1D but matching PaleoMIST.

**Carlson et al. 2012** (EPSL 315–316, 24, doi:10.1016/j.epsl.2011.07.008; read) — Anslow EMBM on 50 km ICE-5G at
15 and 14.5 ka (and a retracted 14 ka test), forced by TraCE-21ka 50-yr means; lapse rates −5 K/km, 0.1/km
(precipitation); fixed albedo snow 0.8, ice 0.5. LIS SMB −0.79 cm/yr SLE at 15 ka (≈ −2900 Gt/yr, inferred),
−1.38 in the Bølling (≈ −5000), −0.73 on the retracted geometry. Ablation ~4 m/yr on the southern and NW margins; the
dry Keewatin dome loses more to sublimation than it accumulates.

**Ullman et al. 2015** (Nat. Geosci. 8, 534, doi:10.1038/ngeo2463; abstract) — energy balance forced by ModelE2-R
time slices 24–9 ka on ICE-5G: LIS SMB positive through most of the deglaciation (retreat mainly dynamic), negative
only in the early Holocene; final deglaciation needed +6–7 °C summer warming and +16–20 W m-2.

**Koepnick, Fu & Tziperman 2024** (EGUsphere, doi:10.5194/egusphere-2024-1998; preprint, one referee rejected it) —
iTraCE (iCESM1.3), ICE-6G; offline SEB melt from CLM. SMB implies LIS loss 2–4× faster than ICE-6G at 20–16 ka, tracks
ICE-6G at 13–12 ka. Melt is a small residual of SW and LW terms each ~8× larger: +1.9 % albedo, −1.45 % LW↓ or +40 %
snowfall removes the misfit.

Interactive, PDD: Gregoire et al. 2012 (Nature 487, 219, doi:10.1038/nature11257) and 2016 (GRL 43, 9130,
doi:10.1002/2016GL070356) — Laurentide–Cordilleran saddle collapse, 5.7–11 m SLE in 340 yr; size set by the PDD factor
and the lapse rate.

## 3. Deglaciation and MIS3

- The sign of the Laurentide SMB through the deglaciation is disputed: positive until the early Holocene (Ullman 2015)
  vs strongly negative from the LGM (Carlson 2012, Koepnick 2024, Bradley 2024). Likely causes (inferred): GCM summer
  SW/LW and cloud biases, the dry ICE-5G dome, albedo choices, and the small melt residual.
- Eurasia (Patton et al. 2017, QSR 169, 148): loss 750 Gt/yr on average at 14.9–12.9 ka, peaks > 3000 Gt/yr, about
  half surface melt.
- MIS3: Niu et al. 2024 (Nat. Geosci. 17, 440, doi:10.1038/s41561-024-01419-z): high summer insolation until ~32 ka
  prevents marked Laurentide growth. No MIS3 SMB on an imposed geometry was found.

## 4. Lessons for an energy-balance SMB

1. Albedo dominates; a Greenland-tuned albedo does not transfer to the Laurentide (Koepnick, Sherriff-Tadano, Gandy).
2. Snow ageing and dust are needed to deglaciate (Willeit & Ganopolski 2018, CP 14, 697; Bauer & Ganopolski 2014,
   CP 10, 1333).
3. A single PDD set cannot match an energy balance across inception, termination and the two continents (Bauer &
   Ganopolski 2017, CP 13, 819); insolation supplies 20–50 % of Eemian melt anomalies (Robinson & Goelzer 2014, TC 8,
   1419; van de Berg et al. 2011, Nat. Geosci. 4, 679); dEBM (Krebs-Kanzow et al. 2021, TC 15, 2295).
4. Clouds and LW↓ matter as much as albedo (Koepnick; Löfverström & Liakka 2016 for ice-free Alaska).
5. Weaker glacial lapse rate (~5 K/km) and the elevation feedback (Abe-Ouchi et al. 2007, CP 3, 423).
6. Refreezing retains 40–50 % of melt near the ELA (Bradley).
7. Daily means miss nonlinear melt; the MPI scheme uses hourly forcing.
8. Resolution and stationary waves set southern-margin temperatures (Sherriff-Tadano).
9. Sea ice controls Fennoscandian accumulation (Simon 2023).

## 5. Our 20 ka run against the literature

Our run (final tuning, LGM snapshot; grounded ice): North ≈ +3.9–4.1 kGt/yr; Laurentide +1616, Fennoscandia +1251,
Barents-Kara +222, Canadian Arctic Archipelago +384, Greenland +869, British Isles −562, Cordillera −131 Gt/yr; total
melt ≈ 12 kGt/yr; land-terminating basins −0.2 to −0.4 kGt/yr.
- Positive Laurentide total: with Ullman 2015 and most PMIP3 models, against Bradley 2024 and Koepnick 2024.
- Southern-margin ablation > 3 m/yr, British-Irish strongly negative, Barents-Kara dry and positive, Cordillera
  negative: consistent.
- Greenland +869 Gt/yr: ~2.3× Kapsch (380) and above Bradley (~500–600, inferred); the difference is accumulation.
- Fennoscandia +1251: large next to Barents-Kara; western accumulation is sea-ice sensitive (Simon 2023).
- Deglacial minimum ≈ −5 kGt/yr at 12.5 ka: the order of Carlson's Bølling value, later in time.
- MIS3 negative: qualitatively as Niu et al. 2024.
